Generated by `scripts/report_eval.py` from `artifacts/eval10_run/` (40 runs: 10 simulation seeds x {sumo_actuated, fixed_time, model A, model B}, 07:00-08:00,

"Beaten" = the 95% t-interval of the per-seed difference excludes zero on the good side AND the exact sign test is significant (9 or 10 of 10 seeds).
"Better (interval only)" = the interval excludes zero but the sign test does not. The full write-up with the per-seed chart is the shared project report.

**Headline.** Both models beat SUMO actuated on lag (-41.4 s and -36.9 s, 10 of 10 seeds each), time loss, backlog, waiting time (-41% / -40%), queue, speed,
due completion, trips completed (+258 / +206), throughput, trip duration and overdue cars; model A also beats it on depart delay. Cars never inserted is level
(501 / 481 vs 503) and teleports are too noisy to call (7.5 / 10.0 vs 13.3, 6 of 10 seeds). No measure is worse. `fixed_time` is worse than SUMO actuated on 11 of 14.

## What changed to beat the baseline (plain English)

1. Fixed the learning: the part that learns *what to do* was almost switched off by the part that learns *how good a situation is*; each now learns at its own pace, rewards were shrunk, and there are 4x more learning steps per simulated hour.
2. Changed the reward: from "waiting time went down" (which rewarded flipping lights) to a small cost every 5 s for each stopped car, each car waiting to enter the city, and each light change.
3. Cleaned up the inputs: dropped two numbers stuck at their maximum; added which phase is green, how long it has run versus its own minimum / maximum, cars waiting per phase, and neighbouring junctions' queues.
4. Let it see the whole queue: stop lines hold ~2 cars per lane while the real queue stands 100-200 m behind; it now counts stopped cars on the road behind, up to 300 m.
5. Let it see cars waiting outside the city, then trained 10-20 more rounds: this closed the last gap, cars that never got in.
